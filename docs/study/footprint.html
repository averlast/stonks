Reading the footprint

A candle tells you where price ended. A footprint tells you what it cost to get there. That difference is the whole read — and it only matters at your levels.

01 What you are looking at

Split a candle open. Inside is a ladder of price rows. Each row shows two numbers — the volume that traded at that price from aggressive sellers hitting the bid, and from aggressive buyers lifting the offer. Under the bar sits its delta: buys minus sells for the whole candle.

One 5-minute candle, opened up

23,46814×96
23,466180×742
23,464410×515
23,462356×330
23,460205×148

bar delta +808 · total 2,996

Read top-down like a price ladder. Left = sellers, right = buyers. The bolded row is where most of the volume traded. Nothing here means anything yet — this is just the anatomy.

Absolute numbers never travel. 742 contracts is meaningless on its own — it is big on a quiet Tokyo open and nothing at the NY cash open. Only the shape within the moment counts: volume versus progress, this bar against the last few.

02 The rule that comes before every read

The footprint only means something at a level. ORB high/low, IB edges, pre-Tokyo range, overnight and London extremes, session VWAP, prior-session value areas. In the middle of nowhere it is noise with good typography — ignore it completely.

Same discipline the whole playbook runs on. The level tells you where to look; the footprint tells you what happened when price got there.

03 Absorption — the fade tell

This is the failed auction, visible frame by frame instead of waiting for a 30-minute close. Price tests a level from below. Watch the top rows.

1,910 contracts lifted the offer at the high — and price went nowhere. The row above barely prints. Somebody passive is selling them everything they ask for. Big volume, no progress.

The same prices now print heavy sellers and the delta flips negative while price is still up here. Everyone who bought that breakout is underwater and starting to puke. That is your fade fuel.

trapped buyers

The checklist: heavy one-sided volume at the extreme + no price progress = the move is hollow. Then the confirmation: next bar, same prices, delta flips.

Mirror it exactly for a low — heavy sell volume at the bottom rows that fails to push price down, then buyers appearing at the same prices.

04 The thin top — the quieter exhaustion

Same conclusion, opposite appearance. Instead of huge volume being absorbed, there is barely any volume at all.

Nobody left to buy

23,4966×11
23,4949×24
23,486402×690
23,484380×445

bar delta +383 · high 23,496

Price poked into fresh air and found no one. The high was made on 35 contracts. There is no participation up there to defend it, so nothing holds it.

hollow break

Absorption means the buyers were met. A thin top means they never turned up. Either way the level held for a reason and the break is not real.

05 Initiative — the continuation tell

The opposite read, and the one that stops you fading a real breakout.

The pullback prints light volume — no one is selling into it — then buy imbalances reappear right at the retested level. Buyers are not defending, they are still paying up. This is your entry context.

The checklist: stacked imbalances through the level + a light-volume retest that holds = the move is real. Fade this and you get run over.

06 What it does not do

  • It is not an entry trigger. It is permission, exactly like the lamp and the cloud. You still enter on your own confirmation — the close, the volume, the engulfing.
  • Absorption can lose. Sometimes the absorber gets run over and price rips through anyway. That is precisely why it is permission and not a trigger.
  • It cannot see waiting walls — only walls being hit. Passive size sitting there before price arrives is Bookmap's heatmap, deferred by decision. One consolation: icebergs show up here as absorption, so hidden defenders invisible to the order book do become visible.
  • Numbers do not compare across days — or across sessions. Tokyo volume and NY-open volume are different worlds.

07 Which tool, which day

Day typeExhaustion readWhy
Trend dayFootprint onlyRSI pins at 70 and stays there. Absorption in the rows is the only honest "this is ending" read.
