How to read the Liquidity Map

It draws where other people's stops are sitting. That's all it draws. Where the fuel is, never what to do about it.

01 The idea it implements

Patterns are liquidity maps, never entry signals.

Classical patterns "work" not because shapes predict prices, but because enough traders act on them identically. A shape everyone recognizes implies a knowable cluster of entries and stops — it tells you who is positioned where. Stops are forced orders. Forced orders are fuel. Fuel is where price gets pushed.

So the map answers two questions, and neither of them is "should I buy here":

  • Offensive — where is the pool the market is likely to run before the real move, so the run becomes your trigger instead of your stop-out?
  • Defensive — when you're already in, which incoming shakeout is expected collection of other people's stops (hold through it), and where must your stop sit so that collection can't reach it?

Your entries do not change. They remain the six setups: sweep-reclaim, breakout & retest, range fade, failed auction, retracement continuation, S/D rejection. The map is a layer underneath them that says which levels are worth caring about and what just happened at one.

The long-form reasoning lives in docs/study/patterns-as-liquidity.md. This page is organized around what's actually on the screen. For what you're permitted to trade at any moment, that's the other instrument: permission-lamp.html.

02 Equal-extremes lines (EQH / EQL)

What a line appearing means

Two or more confirmed pivots landed at roughly the same price — within a fraction of ATR14 — so the indicator drew a line there and labeled it. That price is now a shared reference that everyone with a chart can see. Under it (for equal lows) sits a growing pool: protective sell-stops from everyone long the "double bottom", plus sell-stop entries from everyone waiting to short the break. Mirror it above equal highs.

Equal, untouched lows are a pending event, not support. The line is not telling you the price will hold. It is telling you there is a reason for price to go there.

What the touch count means

The label reads EQL ×3 or EQH ×4. That number does two jobs at once:

  • Every touch grows the pool beyond the level — more stops, more resting breakout orders, a bigger prize.
  • It feeds the third-test rule directly. Three or more tests of the same edge reads as Erosion: passive orders being consumed, not defended. Fading that edge is banned, and the break through it becomes the favored trade.

So a rising count is not a strengthening level. Classically "more touches = stronger"; here it is the opposite once you hit three. Read the count as a countdown.

The two ways a line dies

Price pokes beyond the level by more than the tolerance. Exactly one of two things happens on that bar's close, and the difference is the whole point of the indicator.

A dotted frozen line is still useful — it's where the pool was, and a retest of it from the other side is a Breakout & retest candidate. But it is finished as an active level; the indicator will not touch it again.

Which one to expect: the bounce-strength gradient

The indicator prints the verdict after the fact. Reading it before is your job, and the read is the shape of the bounces off the level so far.

Tempo separates them cleanly: a Liquidity sweep is one fast wick with a sharp reclaim; Erosion is slow, repeated and shallow. The spring-vs-erosion test is the entry gate that enforces it — a sweep-reversal entry needs the close back inside within one or two candles on the signal timeframe, with Confirmation. A slow crawl back inside is not a spring, and there is no trade.

Trend context outranks the gradient. Pools sitting in the auction's path — equal lows under a falling session VWAP — get ridden through, not defended. The defended-spring read belongs to pools swept against the session's direction. This is where the two instruments meet: the map says a pool is there, the Permission lamp says which way the day is allowed to go, and that combination is what tells you whether the sweep is your entry or someone else's.

Defensive corollary: long from a spring, and equal lows later form above your entry? Other people's stops now cluster there, and a dip to collect them is normal, not a thesis break — which is why your stop lives beyond the original sweep extreme, per the Stop anchor rule, and not below the new lows.

03 Gap boxes

The box spans prior RTH close → today's RTH open: a price band that nobody traded during regular hours, with the prior close sitting at its far edge as a magnet Level.

The trapped-crowd read. A gap up traps the overnight shorts: their buy-stops sit overhead, so there is fuel above. At the same time it attracts fill-faders selling toward yesterday's close, so there is a pull below. Two crowds, opposite directions, and the first ~30 minutes decides which one pays:

  • Initiative away from the gap = gap-and-go. Don't fade it. The fill-faders become the fuel.
  • Rotation back into the zone = the fill is on, and the prior close is your target.

Small gaps in balance conditions fill routinely. Trend-day gaps don't. An unfilled gap stays a standing magnet — for the rest of the session and often for days, which is why the indicator keeps several of them on the chart at once.

04 Fair value gaps (FVG / iFVG)

A shaded band where a 15-minute, 30-minute or 1-hour impulse skipped a stretch of prices: three candles in a row where the first and third don't overlap, and the middle one is a real body, not a wick. Green = a bullish impulse left it (the band is where buyers stepped in), red = bearish. The band reads as where the move began — the precise spot a pullback is expected to pause, sharper than "somewhere near the 50%".

What the numbers say (four years of NQ and gold 1-minute bars, first retest inside your trading window): a gap holds its first pullback about 7 points more often than a same-size band dropped at a random price nearby on the 15-minute, about 14 points more on the 1-hour. That's a modest, real lean, not a trigger. And the thing most FVG material insists on, a gap "in confluence" with a level, adds nothing: a gap sitting on PDH, an ORB edge or VWAP held slightly less often than a gap standing alone. So the indicator draws every qualifying gap the same and does not highlight the ones near a level. The write-up is in analysis/fvg_results.md.

How a band changes state

  • Alive (green / red). Extends right every bar. Price can dip into it and hold any number of times; a touch does not spend it.
  • Flipped (purple, dashed, labelled iFVG). A 15-minute bar closed fully beyond the band's far edge. Everyone who bought the pullback into a green band is now trapped, so on the first return the band acts as resistance instead of support (mirror for red). This is the "portends a reversal" read: the return into a purple band is where the trapped crowd gets out, and where a fade in the direction of the failure is looked at. The trade is never the close-through itself, only the return.
  • Retired (grey, frozen). Three ways: it has been tested three or more times without flipping (the third-test rule, it's eroding, expect it to give way); it flipped and price has now come back once (the crowd is out, it's just a price again); or it's older than today plus the prior day.

Timing. Bands are read from closed higher-timeframe bars only, so a 15-minute gap appears on the first chart bar after its third candle closes, and a flip appears on the first chart bar after the 15-minute close that caused it. They never repaint. Cost: up to one HTF bar of lag. A timeframe is skipped when the chart is slower than it.

05 What the indicator deliberately does not do

No named-pattern detection. No head & shoulders, no cups, no channels, no auto-drawn trendlines. That was a decision, not a backlog item: the shapes are subjective, detectors fire late and false, and under the maps-only rule the pools are the signal, not the shapes. Anything a pattern name would have told you is already visible as a line or a box.

The translations, so you can do it in your head when a pattern name surfaces:

The classical name → what it actually is on this map.
Textbook patternWhat it really is
Double / triple bottom or top Equal extremes — already drawn, already counted. The pool grows with every touch; the one that pays is the one whose second low undercuts the first and reclaims. Read the bounce gradient, not the number of touches.
Head & shoulders The head is the sweep. First peak leaves stops above it; the head is the push through them that fails and reclaims — that sweep-reclaim was the short entry, long before the shape has a name. Right shoulder = confirmation, nothing to trade. Neckline sellers park buy-stops just above the third peak, so a bounce up into that pocket after the pattern completes is expected collection, not failure. Your stop belongs above the head's sweep extreme, beyond the whole formation, where the shakeout can't reach it.
Triangle / pennant / flag / wedge All of them are the Coil, and the Coil rule governs: never positioned inside. Trade it as a Liquidity-sweep reversal of the far side, or as a Breakout & retest after a clean break that ran without a shakeout. Special case: a bull flag's low is a favorite single dip-collection point — the flush through it right before the continuation the flag crowd was waiting for.
Pin bar / hammer / shooting star A one-candle sweep: the wick is the run through the level, the close-back is the reclaim. Meaningful only at a Level — noise in open space. Engulfing candles are already a Confirmation element.
Cup & handle / rounding bottom The mirror of Erosion — higher lows with weakening selling, sellers being absorbed — plus a Coil for the handle. Read the gradient and the coil; skip the name.
Measured-move projections Predictable take-profit clusters, so modest magnets. Useful for placing your targets. Never a reason to enter.

06 Known limitations

  • Pivots confirm late. A pivot needs pivotLen bars on each side, so a level appears pivotLen bars after the price that made it. On a 5-bar setting that's five bars of hindsight, every time. The line is never news.
  • The swept-vs-broken verdict is a bar-close call. On the live bar it can flicker — a candle that's currently "swept" is one tick from being "broken" and back again. Wait for the close, or don't use it for that decision.
  • Levels are capped and retired. Only so many active levels per side; the oldest gets frozen when the cap is hit. A lone pivot waiting for a partner expires after candExpiry bars. Absence of a line does not mean absence of a pool.
  • It is a map, not an entry signal. Everything above assumes you already know which setups are legal — and the map has no opinion on that whatsoever.

Where the two instruments split: level-scoped vetoes (the third-test/Erosion ban at a specific edge, the Coil rule at a specific structure) are read here, off per-level evidence. Regime-scoped permission (which side, which family, press or harvest) is read on the Permission lamp. You are the join.

07 Inputs quick reference

One instrument-agnostic script: tradingview/liquidity-map.pine. Runs on the index and gold charts alike; only the RTH session input changes.

Equal extremes

Pivot length (bars each side)
Default 5, minimum 2. Bigger = fewer, more significant levels and a longer confirmation lag. This is the main "how noisy is my map" dial.
Equal tolerance (× ATR14)
Default 0.25. Two pivots count as equal when their prices are within this fraction of ATR14. The same band decides pokes: within it is a touch, beyond it ends the level as a sweep or a break.
Max active levels per side
Default 8. When exceeded, the oldest level is frozen. Keeps the chart readable.
Unpaired pivot expiry (bars)
Default 500. How long a lone pivot waits for an equal partner before it stops being a candidate.
Mark sweeps
On. Prints the "EQH sweep" / "EQL sweep" marker on a wick-through that closes back inside. Breaks stay silent by design.
Show labels / label size / colors
Cosmetic. Labels carry the touch count — leave them on.

Gaps

RTH session (ET)
Index (NQ/ES): 0930-1600. Gold: e.g. 0820-1330. The prior close is taken from the last bar of this session, and the gap is measured to the next session's open — set it wrong and every box is wrong.
Min gap size (points)
Default 0 = draw any gap. Raise it if the tiny overnight drifts clutter the chart.
Max unfilled gaps kept
Default 5. Oldest box is dropped past the cap.

Fair value gaps

15m / 30m / 1h
Which higher timeframes to scan. All on by default. A timeframe is skipped when the chart is slower than it.
Middle candle body
Default 0.5: the middle candle's body must be at least half its range. Filters out three sloppy candles that merely fail to overlap.
Min gap height (× ATR14 of the gap's timeframe)
Default 0.5, the study setting. Lower shows more, smaller bands; the study found no setting that changed the conclusion.
Sessions kept
Default 2 = today plus the prior day. Older bands retire grey.
Max live gaps
Default 12. Oldest band retires past the cap. Shares the box budget with the RTH gap boxes.
Colors
Green bullish, red bearish, purple flipped. Grey is not a setting: it means retired.
