Position sizing

Sizing does not improve your edge. It decides how much of your edge you live long enough to collect. On a prop account with a fixed drawdown, that is the whole game — and it is one number.

01 The one number

Everything reduces to how many full losses the account can absorb before it dies:

NR = account drawdown ÷ risk per trade.

A $2,000 drawdown risking $80 a trade is NR = 25. That is the only sizing number worth carrying around.

Correction, 30 August 2026 — read this before using any $80 on this page.

The arithmetic above is right. The way this page then used it was wrong, and the $80 it produced has been retired.

NR is a property of the pair (stop, account). This page solved it the wrong way round — it took the $2,000 drawdown as fixed and divided to get the stop. Do the opposite: set the stop from what the instrument actually needs, then choose an account size that can hold it. Solving for the stop puts it inside the market's noise, which lowers expectancy, which makes ruin worse — the formula ends up destroying the edge it exists to protect.

Measured on NQ's 9:45 opening-range break (upside breaks only, 2× target, flat 12:30, 529 real trades — analysis/study_nq_breakdown.py): an $80 stop earns $2.77 a trade at a 37% win rate, while a $200 stop earns $16.10 at 53%. Same rule, same days. The $80 was strangling winners before they moved.

The second error: NR ≥ 25 was calibrated for a 500–1000 trade lifetime. A rule that fires ~106 times a year needs far less room than the thumb demands. Bootstrap the real trade distribution instead — analysis/study_nq_survival.py.

It is not "how many losses in a row would kill me" — wins interleave and rebuild the cushion. It is the depth of hole the account can tolerate over its whole life. The chance of ever digging that hole, given a real edge, falls away exponentially as NR grows.

Halving your size squares your ruin probability. Read across the top row: 48% at NR=4 becomes 23% at NR=8 — and 0.48² = 0.23, exactly. Halve again and it squares again. Sizing is not a linear dial. Small changes at the safe end do almost nothing; small changes at the dangerous end do everything.

02 Why the target is 25

A winning strategy still digs holes. Over a few hundred trades, chance alone produces a drawdown deeper than feels reasonable:

A normal year hands you a 15–20R hole whether you deserve it or not. If the account only tolerates 12R, the strategy never gets to finish being right.

NR ≥ 25 wherever the account is worth more than its replacement cost. Below 15 you are gambling on a strategy you believe in. Below 5 the strategy is irrelevant — it is a coin flip with extra steps.

03 Two clocks — why runners beat sizing down

There are two ways to fail: blow up, or take so long it stops mattering. Sizing trades one against the other. Expectancy improves both.

LeverBlow-up riskTime to target
Cut sizebetterworse
Raise expectancybetterbetter

Expectancy is what you make per trade, in R:

  • 40% win rate at 2R → 0.20R per trade
  • 35% win rate at 3R → 0.40R per trade

Win less often, make twice as much per trade, reach the target in half the time, and be slightly safer doing it — at identical risk per trade and identical drawdown room. It is the only lever here with no downside.

The runner test. Of the trades that reach 2R, do more than 3 in 4 also reach 3R? If yes, stop banking at 2R. If no, keep banking at 2R.

Why three quarters: a 2R target earns 3·P₂ − 1, a 3R target earns 4·P₃ − 1. They break even at P₃ = 0.75·P₂.

This is measurable from your own fills — not a judgement call. It is also the one number that would replace the assumed win rate everything else on this page rests on.

04 The eval and the funded account want opposite sizes

Identical rules, opposite correct answers, because the cost of failure is not the same:

Cost of losing itCorrect sizeOptimise for
Eval$115 and a few days4–5 MNQspeed
Fundedthe income stream1 MNQsurvival

They are separate accounts, so nothing forces one size across both. All the survival discipline belongs on the funded side. On the eval side it is just an expensive way to be slow.

Name the eval a funding operation, not trading. Two weeks of deliberately reckless sizing is a habit that then has to be switched off. Making the switch a written rule rather than a feeling is the reason this page exists.

Why the blitz works even with an unproven edge

The eval asks for +$3,000 before −$2,000. For a zero-edge coin flipper, the chance of reaching the target first is 2,000 ÷ 5,000 = 40%. Size up far enough and skill stops mattering — you converge on that floor. The worst case is bounded, so the plan does not depend on the playbook being proven yet.

05 The Lucid Pro 50k rules, and where they bite

Eval
$115 one-time, no monthly fee. +$3,000 target, $2,000 max loss, $1,200 daily loss limit. No consistency rule, no minimum days.
Drawdown
End-of-day trailing, locking at the $50,000 start once peak EOD balance reaches $52,000.
Funded DLL
$1,200 below the initial trail; above it, 60% of (peak EOD balance − $50,000).
Payout
$500 minimum, no upper limit. 40% consistency. Three minimum trading days.

EOD trailing means no cushion until the lock

Before peak EOD reaches $52,000, every dollar made raises the floor by the same dollar. The cushion is always $2,000, no matter how well you trade. Intraday gains do not raise the floor — only closing green does — so handing profit back inside a session is free, while closing green and losing it tomorrow is not.

After the lock, every dollar not withdrawn is permanent room. That is the first moment the account starts getting safer instead of just richer.

The 60% DLL is a trap dressed as a perk. At a $56,000 peak it permits a $3,600 loss in one day — against $6,000 of total room. The rule allows roughly four times more daily risk than is survivable. It is a ceiling to stay far away from, never a budget.

40% consistency

Best day ≤ 40% of total profit, so total must be at least 2.5× the best day. That forces at least three profitable days, which is why the separate 3-day rule is redundant. At 1 MNQ it never binds — it only bites if you have one monster day, which is the thing to avoid anyway.

Cushion beats size, quadratically

Once the floor locks, expected lifetime payout scales with the square of the cushion held, but only linearly with size. Doubling the cushion quadruples what the account pays out over its life; halving the size only doubles it.

Build to roughly $2,000 of cushion before taking the $500 minimum payout, then withdraw the excess and hold the cushion there.

06 The rules, short

  1. Eval: 4–5 MNQ, 40 point max stop. All five copy-traded. Expect about two weeks and about $1,000 all-in. It is a funding operation, not trading.
  2. Funded: 1 MNQ, $200 max risk (corrected 30 Aug 2026 — was $80, see the box in section 01). The day it funds, the contract count drops to one. The stop does not drop with it: $200 is what the instrument needs, and cutting to $80 costs about $13 a trade.
  3. Pick the account to fit the stop, not the stop to fit the account. At a $200 stop on 1 MNQ, a year of NQ upside breaks survives 73% on a 50k, 93% on a 100k, 99% on a 150k. If the honest stop beyond the sweep extreme sits outside the budget band, the trade is illegal — skip the setup, never tighten the stop. A size veto, same standing as the direction and family locks.
  4. Income comes from more accounts, not more contracts. One micro returns roughly $2,100–2,800 per surviving account-year whatever the account size — size buys survival, not earnings. Five funded accounts at one contract beat one account at five.
  5. Optimise dollars per YEAR, not per trade. Trades are the scarce resource: this rule fires at most once a morning, so ~253 a year taking both sides and ~132 taking only the better one. The higher-quality upside-only trade earns more each time ($16.43 versus $11.98) and less each year ($2,143 versus $3,029). Per-trade quality is the wrong objective when the trade count is capped.
  6. Step up only from ahead, never to catch up. Raising size while behind is the martingale, and it is how a 20% risk becomes a 50% one.
  7. Build $2,000 of cushion before the first payout. Ignore the 60% DLL.
  8. Measure the runner test. If more than three in four 2R touches reach 3R, move the target out and take the free half of the calendar.

What a bad month means: nothing. At 40% @ 2R over 100 trades, an eight-loss streak is a coin flip and a ten-loss streak happens one run in five. It takes roughly 216 trades — about seven months at ~390 a year — before a losing stretch carries any information at all. The correct response to a bad month is not to change the strategy.
