How to read the Permission lamp

It is a discipline instrument, not an advisor. It tells you what you may not do, and why. Everything it stays quiet about is still your job.

01 What it is — and what it refuses to do

The Permission lamp is a passive table sitting on the chart. It shows two things: the current regime state, and which of the six setup archetypes are legal right now, each with a reason. That's the whole surface.

It will never say "enter". It will never say "conditions met". That silence is the design, not a missing feature.

The asymmetry that decides everything: a wrong veto costs you a trade you would have taken — cheap, and another one is coming. A wrong trigger puts you in a trade you shouldn't be in — expensive, and it is the documented failure mode. Your logged mistakes are trades the rules banned, not trades the rules allowed and you skipped.

There's a second reason, and it matters more. The playbook's rules — the Auction bias gate, the Entry gates, the Coil rule, the two-strike switch — exist as pre-commitments. They were written on the assumption that in-session you will want to do the wrong thing, and that wanting is the thing being defended against. So the tool built on top of them cannot depend on your self-control to be safe. A lamp that only vetoes fails safe: the worst it can do to you on a bad day is stand you down when you didn't need standing down.

Full reasoning, including what was rejected and why: docs/adr/0011-permission-lamp-vetoes-only.md.

02 The side state — four values, plain words

Everything starts with one question from the Auction bias gate: which side of the session VWAP is price on, and which way is VWAP sloping? Two facts, four possible answers.

Stand-by, said properly

Price above a falling VWAP, or below a rising one, means the auction is mid-argument. It is either a reclaim attempt that's about to turn the day, or a pullback stretching before the trend resumes — and which one it is gets decided by what happens next, not by anything visible now. So: no directional setups and no chop fades. Chop fades here are knife-catches, which is exactly why disagreement was never folded into chop.

You will notice the lamp goes dark during every VWAP reclaim — including the ones that become the day's best trend-change entry. That is deliberate. Trading the reclaim itself is a judgment read at structure, and the lamp was never licensed to authorize judgment. If you take it, you take it on your own authority, knowingly, with the lamp saying nothing. That is allowed. Telling yourself the lamp agreed with you is not.

Stand-by is short-lived by construction: price returns to its side, or the slope gives in. Wait it out.

03 Ambition — press or harvest, and the unanimity rule

The side state says what you may trade. The higher timeframes say how greedily you may manage it. They never override the side state — a bull 4h does not make longs legal below a falling VWAP. They set ambition only.

  • Press — scale out at the first magnets, keep a runner, trail it toward the far targets.
  • Harvest — nearest magnet stack, take the money, no runner, reduced size.

Press requires all three arrows to agree: the day's direction (the session VWAP side state), the HTF SMA regime on the 1h (50 vs 200), and the same on the 4h. Any disagreement anywhere — HTF against the session, or a 1h/4h split — is harvest. That is the unanimity rule, and it has no exceptions.

Worked example: a short day (price below a falling session VWAP).
Session side1h regime4h regimeAmbition
short-only ↓bear ↓bear ↓press — runner allowed
short-only ↓bear ↓bull ↑harvest — the 4h disagrees
short-only ↓bull ↑bear ↓harvest — 1h/4h split
short-only ↓bull ↑bull ↑harvest — HTF against the day

One row in four is press. That's the point: press stays rare enough to carry information. Timeframe disagreement is exactly where Two-sided runs live, and no tactical stop distance survives one — so disagreement buys you smaller size and a nearer target, automatically.

The accepted cost, so it doesn't surprise you. The 4h lags genuine regime turns. The first monster day of a new trend will therefore read harvest — the exact day a runner would have paid best. You forfeit the runner's tail on that one day. You never forfeit the trade. Press-in-error (holding a runner into a disagreeing 4h) costs far more than harvest-in-error, which is why the trade-off is priced this way.

When the side state is chop, ambition is undefined and the lamp says so — chop rules already fix the behavior (fades, near targets, no runner).

04 Setup legality — two locks, one table

Legality is not a hand-written list. It composes from two independent locks:

  1. Direction lock — from the side state. long-only permits long setups; short-only permits short setups; chop permits both directions; stand-by permits neither.
  2. Family lock — from the same state, applied to the Setup families. Chop bans the continuation family (Breakout & retest, Retracement continuation — trades that need a trend to keep going) and permits the fade family (Range trade, Failed auction, Liquidity-sweep reversal, Supply/demand rejection) both ways. Trending states permit both families, direction-locked. Stand-by permits nothing.
Every state × every setup. Nothing in this table is discretionary.

Two readings worth holding on to. First: in a trending state a fade is still legal — but only with the day. Below a falling VWAP you may fade a push back up into supply; you may not fade the lows. Second: "Range trade fades only" in the two-strike rule means the fade family — all four archetypes — not the single Range trade archetype.

Ambition never changes legality. Press and harvest are management. A harvest day and a press day permit exactly the same trades; they differ only in how much you ask of them. If you catch yourself reasoning "the 4h is bullish, so this long is okay" below a falling VWAP, you have just crossed the two wires the lamp exists to keep apart.

05 The one hand-set input: strikes

The indicator cannot see your fills. A live chart doesn't know you got stopped out. So the two-strike regime switch — two stop-outs on the same directional idea reclassifies the day as chop — has to be fed by hand.

The strikes input is 0, 1, or 2. You set it yourself, right after each stop-out, before you do anything else. At 2 the lamp forces the chop state and labels it "two-strike (hand-set)" — so you can always see that this chop came from your keyboard and not from the VWAP. Everything downstream then follows normally: continuation family banned, fades only, or done for the day.

The overnight trap. Hand-set values persist. A 2 you typed yesterday afternoon is still a 2 at tomorrow's open, and it will silently ban half your playbook on a clean trend day. The lamp defends against this by challenging a carried-over nonzero count at the session open — "strikes carried over — reset?" — instead of obeying it. It challenges; it does not clear it for you.

Reset it every morning. Make it part of Prep.

Note what the quarantine buys: exactly one input on the whole lamp is not a judgment-free computation, and it is visibly marked as such everywhere it has an effect. Every other veto you can trust without asking where it came from.

06 What stays yours

The lamp is deliberately small. These remain judgment, and no version of the lamp takes them over:

  • Footprint reads — absorption vs initiative at a Level. The lamp has no aggressor-side data and never will.
  • Whether the auction has finished arguing — the A+ quality call. Sweeps come in sequences until the argument ends, so a first sweep in an unresolved auction is never A+. Nothing computes this.
  • The failed-auction target-map flip — a failed first initiative move turns the pools in the failed direction into supply and the other side's level stack into your target sequence. That is a target change, not a permission change, so it sits outside the lamp on purpose.
  • Level-scoped vetoes — the third-test/Erosion ban at a specific edge, the Coil rule at a specific structure. These are per-Level evidence and they live on the Liquidity Map, not here.
  • Supply/Demand zones — drawn by hand on the higher timeframe, judgment-placed.
  • Sizing, and the choice of Stop anchor policy (tight / structural / conditional — never mixed by halves).

The screen composes. The lamp gives you regime permission. The Liquidity Map gives you per-Level evidence — where the pools are, which edge has been tested three times, what just got swept. You are the join. Neither instrument does the other's job, and neither does yours.

The map's half of this is written up in liquidity-map.html, with the underlying idea in docs/study/patterns-as-liquidity.md.

07 Indicator quick reference

One instrument-agnostic script: tradingview/permission-lamp.pine. Set the anchor session to whatever you're trading.

Session VWAP anchor (ET)
The window the session VWAP anchors to. Index (NQ/ES): 0930-1600. Gold NY metals: 0820-1330. Gold Asia: 1800-0300. Everything else on the lamp keys off this.
VWAP slope lookback
Bars used to measure the slope. Default 10. Open tuning param.
Flat-slope threshold
How flat counts as flat, as a fraction of ATR14. Default ~0.15. Open tuning param — it decides how often you see chop versus a trending state, so change it slowly, and only between sessions.
Chart timeframe
Run it on 1m–5m. Slope is measured in bars, so the same lookback means something different on a 1h chart. Off-timeframe readings are meaningless, not merely noisy.
SMA lengths
50 / 200, evaluated on the 1h and the 4h from developing values. 50 above 200 = bull, 200 above 50 = bear.
Strikes
0–2, hand-set. See section 05.
Table position & size
Cosmetic. Put it somewhere you can't avoid seeing it.

Two states that aren't verdicts

  • OFF — you are outside the anchor session. No side state is being computed, and the absence of a veto here means nothing at all.
  • WARMING UP — the first bars of the session. The slope isn't measurable yet, so no state exists. Same rule: absence of a veto is not permission.

Both of these are the lamp saying "I have nothing", not "you're clear". The lamp's only positive claims are its bans.
